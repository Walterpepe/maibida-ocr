What Is CCD Inline Inspection? High-Speed Inline Vision Explained

In short: CCD inline inspection integrates industrial cameras, lighting and vision algorithms directly into the production line to perform appearance, dimension, character (OCR) and presence checks in real time while products keep moving, instantly outputting pass/fail signals—turning quality control from after-the-fact sampling into 100% online inspection.

1. What CCD Inline Inspection Means

CCD (charge-coupled device) is a common image sensor in industrial cameras. Inline inspection means the device sits on the production line itself: products need no offline handling and are captured and judged while moving on conveyors, stamping or injection. The combination creates a CCD inline system that checks every product 100% and links reject, stop or marking actions in real time.

The fundamental difference from offline inspection is that inspection runs in sync with production, so defects are caught the moment they occur and batches of bad parts are prevented from escaping.

2. System Components and Workflow

A typical CCD inline system has four parts—imaging, control, algorithm and execution:

  1. Imaging unit: industrial camera, lens and tailored lighting for clear, high-contrast images of moving products;
  2. Trigger and control: encoder or photoelectric sensor triggers capture as each part arrives, synchronized with line pace;
  3. Vision algorithms: positioning, dimensional measurement, appearance defect detection, OCR and presence checks;
  4. Execution linkage: I/O or communication signals drive reject mechanism, alarm or stop, with data logged.

3. Typical Applications

Connector and stamped strip inspection

Connector terminals are small and high-volume. The CCD system detects terminal deformation, missing pins, burrs, contamination and dimensional deviations while the strip moves at high speed, matching fast stamping cycles.

HDMI strip feeding inspection

For continuous strip feeding of HDMI-type connectors, inline vision monitors pin flatness, coplanarity and appearance defects in real time, promptly revealing mold wear or process drift.

Insert molding inspection

Misaligned, missing or displaced inserts directly cause mold collision or scrap. Inline cameras confirm insert state before and after mold closing, intercepting abnormalities at the source.

Plastic part inline inspection

Detects short fill, flash, sink marks, color difference and printed characters on injection parts, replacing manual visual judgment at high cycle rates.

4. Key Performance Indicators

  • Inspection accuracy: set by camera resolution, field of view and minimum feature size, derived from tolerance requirements;
  • Inspection speed: single-station time must be shorter than the line cycle, using parallel cameras when needed;
  • Misjudgment rate: controlled through lighting optimization, positioning compensation and algorithm thresholds for both misses and false rejects;
  • Data capability: yield statistics, defect classification, alarm records and MES integration to support process improvement.

5. Selection Considerations for Implementation

When planning CCD inline inspection, first define part dimensions and tolerances, defect types, line cycle, installation space and linkage method, then determine camera count, lighting and algorithm functions. Run feasibility tests with real samples at the proposal stage to validate accuracy and stability before line integration.

FAQ

What is the difference between CCD inline and offline inspection?

Inline equipment is integrated into the line and captures/judges moving products in real time, enabling 100% inspection with immediate reject or stop linkage. Offline inspection checks products after they leave the line via sampling or batches, which has a delay and cannot stop bad batches in real time.

What items can CCD inline inspection check?

Appearance defects (short fill, flash, burrs, stains, color difference), dimensional and geometric tolerances, characters and markings (OCR), part presence, and insert positioning. Actual capability depends on camera, lighting and algorithm configuration.

How do cameras capture clearly on a high-speed line?

A photoelectric or encoder signal triggers precisely, combined with short exposure, high-intensity strobe lighting and motion-suitable lenses to freeze motion; multi-station or multi-camera setups finish multiple angles without affecting cycle time.

What happens after an inline defect is found?

The system sends a signal via I/O or fieldbus to the PLC, driving a reject mechanism to divert the bad part, or triggering an alarm/stop, while saving defect images and data for traceability and process analysis.

What should I prepare before adopting CCD inline inspection?

Organize part dimensions/tolerances, defect types and pass criteria, line cycle, installation space and linkage method, and provide real samples for feasibility testing to determine camera resolution, lighting, camera count and line integration.
